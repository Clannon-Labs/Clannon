# Skill: checking a claim against its source

- Pin the EXACT claim — the specific figure, date, name, or quote. Vague
  assertions can't be verified; isolate what is actually being asserted.
- Fetch the CITED source and check it literally states the claim, not something
  adjacent. A source that does not contain the claim is MISATTRIBUTED; a URL that
  is fabricated or unreachable is a HALLUCINATED citation.
- Corroborate from a SECOND independent source before calling a claim SUPPORTED.
  Two pages that trace to the same origin are not independent corroboration.
- Check currency: a claim true a year ago may be stale now. Note the source's date
  and flag time-sensitive facts.
- If sources disagree, mark CONTRADICTED and present both positions with
  attribution; do not silently pick one.
- Never invent supporting evidence to pass a claim. If it can't be verified from a
  real retrieved source, say so and lower confidence.
