# Skill: condensing content

A method for turning long content into a faithful short form.

## 1. Read for the spine first

Before writing, find the through-line: what is this actually about, what was decided
or concluded, and what does the reader need from it. Everything else is detail that
either supports the spine or can be cut.

## 2. Keep what's load-bearing

Preserve: decisions and their rationale, conclusions, concrete numbers/dates/names,
action items and owners, and any explicit caveats or risks. These are the things a
reader would be wrong without.

## 3. Cut what isn't

Drop: repetition, throat-clearing, tangents, examples that only illustrate an already-
clear point, and process narration. Shorter is the goal — but never at the cost of a
load-bearing fact.

## 4. Hit the requested shape and length

Produce exactly what was asked: a paragraph, N bullets, an executive summary, a TL;DR.
If a length was specified, meet it. If a focus was specified ("just the decisions",
"only the risks"), filter to it and say what you filtered out.

## 5. Add nothing, sharpen nothing

Summarize only what's in the source. Don't introduce facts, don't turn a "maybe" into
a "will", and don't resolve a contradiction the source left open — flag it instead.

## 6. Write it

Lead with the single most important takeaway, then the rest in descending importance.
Headline it in `summary`; put the full condensed form in `full_content`.
